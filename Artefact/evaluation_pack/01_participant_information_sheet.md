## What is this study about?

Micro-cracks in hatching eggs are difficult to see during routine inspection, and when one is set
for incubation it usually fails to hatch and can contaminate neighbouring eggs. This research has
built a **prototype** that examines a candled egg photograph, reports whether a micro-crack is
present, classifies the type of crack, and suggests whether the likely cause lies with shell
quality or with handling.

The prototype works but is not a product. It currently needs each egg photographed by hand. What
is unknown is whether **the information it produces would be useful to the people who manage egg
quality**, and whether a future automated version would be worth building. That is what this
session is for.

## Why have I been invited?

Because you work as a production or hatchery manager and have practical experience of egg quality
and the decisions that follow from it. Your judgement about whether this tool would help or get in
the way is the thing being measured.

## What will I be asked to do?

One session of about **25 minutes**, at a time and place convenient to you.

- A demonstration of the prototype: one egg examined in detail, then a tray of twenty
- A short explanation of how it might work in future with a camera on the egg belt
- A questionnaire of fifteen statements and five open questions, about eight minutes

**You will not be asked to operate the software.** I am asking for your professional judgement on
whether this kind of information would be useful in your work, not for you to test the interface.

## Is this a test of me?

**No.** Nothing you say is judged. Critical answers are more useful to this research than polite
ones, and a response saying the idea would not work on your farm is a valid and valuable result.
