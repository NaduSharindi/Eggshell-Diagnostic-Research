# Demonstration Script

**Session length** about 25 minutes. One participant at a time, or a small group if diaries force it
— but record which, because a group session produces correlated answers and that must be reported.

## Before you start

- [ ] Consent form signed
- [ ] App running, **Inspect** tab open, no image loaded
- [ ] Three prepared eggs: one clear Star, one faint Hairline, one Intact
- [ ] One prepared tray of 20 images already uploaded in the **Batch** tab, not yet analysed
- [ ] Google Form open on a second device, or paper copies

Keep to the script. If you demonstrate different things to different people, their answers are not
comparable and you cannot pool them.

---

## 1. Context, about 3 minutes

> "Micro-cracks in hatching eggs are hard to see during routine candling. An egg with a micro-crack
> usually fails to hatch, and it can contaminate the eggs around it in the setter.
>
> I have built a prototype that looks at a photograph of a candled egg and does three things: it
> says whether there is a micro-crack, it says what type of crack it is, and it suggests whether
> the likely cause is shell quality or handling.
>
> I am going to show you how it works. Then I want your opinion as someone who does this job — not
> on the software, but on whether this kind of information would be useful to you."

**Say plainly what it is not.** This protects the honesty of the answers:

> "This is a prototype, not a product. Today it needs you to photograph each egg by hand over a
> candling light. The long-term idea is a camera on the egg belt doing it automatically, but that
> is not built. I am asking you to judge the idea and the information it gives, knowing that."

---

## 2. Single egg, about 6 minutes

Upload the **Star** egg. Age class: Peak production.

Point out, in this order:

1. The verdict and the crack probability
2. The crack type, and that it is one of three the system distinguishes
3. The heat map — *"this is where the model says the crack is, so you can check whether it is
   looking at the right thing"*
4. The root-cause split, the interpretation, and the suggested actions

Then the **faint Hairline**, and specifically the low-confidence flag if it fires:

> "When the system is unsure it says so, and tells you to check that egg by hand."

Then the **Intact** egg, briefly.

Invite questions and answer them, but **do not oversell**. If someone asks whether it is accurate,
give the real figures: it misses about 1 cracked egg in 50, and flags about 1 intact egg in 6 for a
check that turns out unnecessary.

---

## 3. Batch, about 6 minutes

Run the prepared tray of 20.

> "This is where it is actually useful. One egg tells you very little. A tray from one flock tells
> you whether your problem is feed or handling."

Show: the crack rate, the morphology mix, the batch root-cause split, and the recommended actions.
Then download the report and let them read it, or hand them a printed copy.

---

## 4. Future direction, about 2 minutes

> "What you have seen needs manual photography. The direction I would like to take it is a camera
> mounted over the egg belt, running this same model automatically, so you get a running crack rate
> and cause breakdown per flock without anyone photographing anything.
>
> That is not built and is not part of this project. I am asking whether it would be worth
> building."

---

## 5. Questionnaire, about 8 minutes

Hand over the form, or send the link.

> "Fifteen statements and a few open questions. There are no right answers, and critical answers
> are more useful to me than polite ones. Please be blunt."

**Leave the room, or at least stop watching.** Standing over someone while they rate your work
inflates every number.
